# Product Art Direction

Load in Phase 4. Find the product's category, then follow its physics.

**The governing idea: you light the material, not the object.** A camera doesn't record a
shampoo bottle — it records light bouncing off glossy plastic. Change the material and every
correct answer changes with it. Gloss wants a big soft source placed to make a controlled
highlight; matte wants diffuse fill and separation by gradient; food wants backlight so steam
and juice read; fabric wants raking light so the weave has relief. Apply the same lighting to
all four and three of them look cheap.

This is the difference between an ad that looks expensive and one that looks generated.

---

## How to use a category entry

Each entry gives you seven things to put into your shot prompts:

| Field | What it decides |
|---|---|
| **Light** | The scheme. Say the source, its size, its position, and what kills spill. |
| **Camera** | Lens, distance, and whether the camera moves or the subject does. |
| **Texture cue** | The physical detail that proves quality. Name it explicitly in the prompt. |
| **Palette** | Colour logic, including what to avoid. |
| **Proof shot** | The one shot that does the persuading for this category. |
| **Pace** | Cut rhythm and total length that suits the category. |
| **Register fit** | Which creative registers work, which fight the product. |

A universal rule that survives every category: **move the camera for big things, move the
object for small things.** A car wants a tracking camera; a lipstick wants a still camera and
a rotating product.

## 1. Glossy packaging — shampoo, shower gel, cosmetics, glass bottles

The hardest common category, because gloss reflects everything including things you don't
want in frame.

- **Light** — One large soft source (big softbox / scrim) placed at 45° camera-left or right,
  high enough that the highlight lands as a *vertical streak* down the bottle's shoulder, not
  a blown circle on its face. **Black flags or black cards on the opposite side** — without
  them the bottle picks up stray reflections and reads as plastic tat. A subtle rim from
  behind separates it from the background.
- **Camera** — 85–100mm equivalent, shallow depth so the label is the only sharp plane.
  Turn the bottle to 3/4, never dead-on: the highlight then describes the curve and the
  object reads as three-dimensional. Camera still, product rotating slowly.
- **Texture cue** — A single controlled highlight streak. Condensation beads if it's cooling
  or fresh. Liquid moving *inside* a translucent bottle sells volume better than any claim.
- **Palette** — Background 1–2 stops darker than the bottle, in a complementary hue. Never
  put a glossy white bottle on a white background without a gradient — it dissolves.
- **Proof shot** — The pour, or the product turning so the highlight travels across the label.
- **Pace** — Unhurried. 1.5–2.5s per shot; 15–20s total. Gloss needs time to read.
- **Register fit** — Straight commercial and arthouse both excel. Humour works only for
  challenger brands. UGC works for the *result*, not the bottle.

Prompt fragment that works:

> Amber glass shampoo bottle, three-quarter angle, single large softbox camera-left creating
> one clean vertical highlight down the shoulder, black flag camera-right killing secondary
> reflections, subtle rim light separating it from a dark charcoal gradient background, 100mm
> macro, shallow depth, label crisp, slow clockwise rotation, no other light sources

## 2. Food and drink — burgers, coffee, ice cream, cocktails

Appetite appeal is physics: heat, moisture, freshness, texture.

- **Light** — **Backlight from behind and above** is the whole game. It makes steam visible,
  puts specular glisten on sauce and juice, and gives translucent things (a drink, a leaf,
  a cheese pull) glow. Bounce a little fill from the front so the shadows aren't dead. Never
  flat frontal light — food dies under it.
- **Camera** — Macro to close. 3/4 elevation for burgers and plated food, dead-level for
  drinks so the liquid line reads. Slow push in. Handheld micro-movement adds life.
- **Texture cue** — Steam, condensation on cold glass, a droplet in motion, sear marks, the
  crumb of a bun, the drag of a spoon. **Motion sells food more than stillness**: pour,
  drip, pull, sizzle, cut.
- **Palette** — Warm grade, but keep whites honest or it looks like cheap fast-food ads.
  Deep shadows for richness. Green herbs and a red element read as "fresh."
- **Proof shot** — The bite, the pour, the cheese pull, the first cut. Universally the
  highest-performing food shot.
- **Pace** — Fast. 0.8–1.5s per shot; 9–15s total. Appetite is an impulse, don't stall it.
- **Register fit** — Humour and UGC both perform strongly. Straight commercial for premium.
  Arthouse only for high-end spirits or chocolate.

Prompt fragment:

> Double cheeseburger on dark slate, strong backlight from behind-above rimming the steam,
> soft bounce fill from camera front, visible sear on the patty, cheese slowly pulling as the
> top bun lifts, macro 60mm, warm grade with honest whites, deep shadows, slow push in,
> shallow depth on the melting cheese

## 3. Matte products — tech, hardware, matte plastic, ceramics, paper goods

The opposite problem to gloss: nothing reflects, so shape must come from gradient.

- **Light** — Broad diffuse toplight, slightly forward. Then a **gradient background** — a
  gentle falloff behind the object — because with no specular highlight, edge contrast is the
  only thing that gives form. One narrow accent light grazing an edge to define it.
- **Camera** — 50–85mm, less shallow than gloss; matte objects want their whole form legible.
  Slow orbit or slow slide reveals form better than a static frame.
- **Texture cue** — The soft-touch surface catching a graze of light. A precise seam or
  chamfer. Fingertip contact proves tactility better than any description.
- **Palette** — Restrained, near-monochrome, one accent that's the brand colour. Cool grades
  suit tech; warm suits ceramics and paper.
- **Proof shot** — The interaction: a hand pressing, a lid closing with weight, a port
  clicking home. Matte products sell on *feel*.
- **Pace** — Medium-slow. 1.5–2s per shot; 15–25s total.
- **Register fit** — Straight commercial is home ground. Arthouse for premium tech. Humour
  rarely fits hardware. UGC is strong for unboxing.

## 4. Fabric and soft goods — clothing, bedding, towels, upholstery

Fabric that isn't moving is a photograph, and a photograph of fabric tells you nothing.

- **Light** — **Raking sidelight**, nearly parallel to the surface, so the weave throws
  micro-shadows and relief appears. Soft fill opposite to keep shadows open. Backlight for
  anything sheer.
- **Camera** — Mix macro on the weave with a wider shot in use. Follow the movement rather
  than holding still.
- **Texture cue** — **Movement is mandatory.** Drape falling, a corner lifting in a breeze,
  a hand running across the pile, fibres catching light as they shift. Motion is the proof
  of quality.
- **Palette** — Let the fabric's own colour dominate; keep surroundings neutral. Natural
  light reads more honest than studio for home textiles.
- **Proof shot** — The drape, or a hand sinking into the pile.
- **Pace** — Slow, sensory. 2–3s per shot; 15–25s total.
- **Register fit** — Arthouse and straight commercial. UGC excellent for bedding and
  apparel. Humour for fast fashion only.

## 5. Large objects — cars, motorcycles, machinery, boats

Too big to move, so the camera does the moving.

- **Light** — Natural, directional, low: golden hour or the blue minutes after sunset. Long
  reflections along body panels are the point — they describe the surface. Wet ground doubles
  every highlight and instantly reads premium.
- **Camera** — **Camera moves, subject stays or drives.** Tracking alongside, low-angle
  approach, slow orbit, drone reveal. Wide lenses for context, long lenses to compress a
  detail. Low camera height makes anything look more imposing.
- **Texture cue** — Reflections travelling along the panel line. Tyre contact with road.
  Paint depth in raking light.
- **Palette** — Environment as co-star: the location sells the aspiration as much as the
  object. Cool blue hour or warm golden hour, rarely midday.
- **Proof shot** — The reveal: the object entering frame, or the camera arriving at it.
- **Pace** — Confident, medium. 2–3s per shot; 20–30s total.
- **Register fit** — Straight commercial and arthouse. Humour for challenger or budget
  brands. UGC weak here — production value *is* the message.

## 6. Interiors and property — apartments, houses, hotels, restaurants

You're selling a feeling of living there, not a floor plan.

- **Light** — Available light, windows visible in frame, warm interior sources balanced
  against cool daylight. Dusk is the highest-value moment: windows glow, interiors read
  warm, the sky is still blue. Never flat overhead lighting.
- **Camera** — Slow dollies through doorways, gentle rises, thresholds. Movement through
  space is what conveys space. Wide but not distorted — fisheye reads as a bad listing.
- **Texture cue** — Light falling across a floor, steam from a cup, a curtain breathing,
  signs of life. **Empty rooms feel like inventory; inhabited rooms feel like home.**
- **Palette** — Warm interior against cool exterior. Let the architecture set the palette.
- **Proof shot** — The threshold moment: the door opening, or arriving at the view.
- **Pace** — Slow, generous. 2.5–4s per shot; 25–45s total.
- **Register fit** — Arthouse and straight commercial. UGC works for rentals and hosts.
  Humour rarely.

## 7. Jewellery, watches, small luxury

Extreme specular control at extreme magnification.

- **Light** — Small hard sources for stones so facets flash; large soft sources for metal so
  it reads smooth. Usually both, carefully separated. **Black surround** to control what the
  metal reflects. Tiny movements of either light or object make facets fire.
- **Camera** — True macro, very shallow. Micro-movements only — at this magnification a
  small move is a big move.
- **Texture cue** — Facet fire, brushed-metal grain, the depth of enamel, a moving second
  hand.
- **Palette** — Near-black or deep jewel-tone surround. Metal and stone are the only bright
  values.
- **Proof shot** — Light travelling across the piece; the clasp closing.
- **Pace** — Very slow. 2–4s per shot; 15–20s total.
- **Register fit** — Arthouse and straight commercial only. Humour and UGC undercut luxury.

## 8. Cosmetics and skincare in use

Two products in one: the object, and the result on skin.

- **Light** — Beauty lighting for skin: large frontal soft source, often with a reflector
  below to open shadows under the eyes. Skin must read smooth and luminous but still like
  skin. For product-only shots, follow the gloss rules above.
- **Camera** — Macro on texture and application; medium close on the face. Symmetrical,
  clean, stable.
- **Texture cue** — Cream spreading, absorbing, a droplet's viscosity, skin catching light
  after application. Before/after in one continuous move is powerful.
- **Palette** — Soft, low-contrast, skin-flattering. Neutral or pale surrounds.
- **Proof shot** — Application: the moment the product meets skin.
- **Pace** — Medium. 1.5–2.5s per shot; 15–25s total.
- **Register fit** — UGC is the strongest performer in this category by a wide margin.
  Straight commercial for premium. Arthouse for fragrance.

## 9. Digital products, apps, SaaS

No physical object, so the interface and the outcome must carry the ad.

- **Light** — The screen is the light source. Ambient environment lit low and cool so the
  UI dominates. If a device is in frame, treat its body as a matte product.
- **Camera** — Move on the UI, don't just cut to it. Slow push into a screen, tilt down to
  a phone. Show a *hand* using it — human presence transforms a screen recording into an ad.
- **Texture cue** — Real data, real motion, a genuine moment of relief when the task
  completes. Never fake numbers.
- **Palette** — The product's own UI palette, extended into the environment.
- **Proof shot** — The outcome: the thing that used to take an hour finishing in seconds.
- **Pace** — Fast. 1–1.5s per shot; 15–30s total.
- **Register fit** — Humour performs unusually well for software. UGC very strong.
  Straight commercial for enterprise. Arthouse almost never.

## 10. Services and experiences — salons, clinics, gyms, courses, travel

Nothing to put on a table, so people and transformation are the product.

- **Light** — Location-appropriate and honest. Over-lighting a service reads as a stock
  photo. Natural window light is the workhorse.
- **Camera** — Observational, moving with people. Faces matter more than rooms.
- **Texture cue** — Genuine reaction — the moment someone sees the result. Micro-expression
  beats any claim.
- **Palette** — Warm and human. Clinical only when clinical is the promise.
- **Proof shot** — The transformation moment, or a real reaction to it.
- **Pace** — Medium. 1.5–2.5s per shot; 20–30s total.
- **Register fit** — UGC dominates. Straight commercial for clinics. Humour for gyms and
  courses. Arthouse for luxury travel.

## 11. Toys, kids' products, pets

Energy and delight, not polish.

- **Light** — Bright, high-key, cheerful. Deep shadows read as ominous. Daylight-balanced,
  lots of fill.
- **Camera** — Get low, to the child's or animal's eye level. Handheld energy is a feature.
  Follow the action rather than composing it.
- **Texture cue** — Real reaction, real motion, slight chaos. Perfection reads as fake.
- **Palette** — Saturated, primary-leaning, playful.
- **Proof shot** — Genuine joy or genuine play.
- **Pace** — Fast, bouncy. 0.8–1.5s per shot; 12–20s total.
- **Register fit** — Humour and UGC. Never arthouse.

## 12. Supplements, pharma, wellness

Credibility is the constraint, and often a legal one.

- **Light** — Clean, bright, even. Slightly cool. Clinical clarity signals trustworthiness.
- **Camera** — Stable, symmetrical, unhurried. Avoid dramatic angles — drama reads as
  overpromising here.
- **Texture cue** — Precision: a measured dose, a sealed packet, a clean surface.
- **Palette** — White, pale blue or green, one accent. Avoid heavy grading.
- **Proof shot** — The routine — the product's place in an ordinary day.
- **Pace** — Measured. 2–2.5s per shot; 15–25s total.
- **Register fit** — Straight commercial. UGC works but raises compliance risk with claims.
  **Check `brand.claims_to_avoid` in the fleet config, and never invent efficacy claims.**

## Category not listed?

Ask which of these the material behaves like: **does it reflect, absorb, or emit light?**

- Reflects sharply → follow **gloss** (§1) or **jewellery** (§7)
- Absorbs → follow **matte** (§3) or **fabric** (§4)
- Emits → follow **digital** (§9)
- Is edible → follow **food** (§2)
- Too big to move → follow **large objects** (§5)
- The product is a person's outcome → follow **services** (§10)

Then adjust pace and register to the price point: the cheaper and more impulsive the
purchase, the faster the cut and the shorter the ad.

## Two failure modes worth naming

**One lighting scheme for everything.** The clearest signal of amateur AI ad work. If your
prompt for a burger and your prompt for a laptop describe the same light, one of them is
wrong.

**Describing mood instead of physics.** "Cinematic, professional, high quality" gives a model
nothing to act on. "Single softbox camera-left, black flag opposite, 100mm, shallow depth,
cool grade" gives it everything. Name the source, its size, its position, and what blocks
the spill.
